01Know your cloth

A guide to fine cotton fabrics

From cambric to chambray, here is how the most common fine cottons are made, how they feel and how to choose between them.

AThe basics

Why weave matters

Every woven fabric is made from two sets of threads: the warp, running lengthways, and the weft, running across. In a plain weave, each weft thread passes alternately over and under each warp thread, creating the simplest and most stable structure.

Cambric, lawn, voile, poplin, batiste and chambray are all plain weaves. What makes them different is the fineness and twist of the yarns, how tightly they are packed together, and how the cloth is finished after weaving.

Thread count, the number of threads in a square inch, gives a rough idea of fineness, but yarn quality and finishing matter just as much. A well-made cotton with a moderate thread count can feel nicer than a poor one with a high count.

CBuying tips

How to judge quality

  • Hold it to the light. The weave should look even, without thin patches or slubs (unless intended).
  • Feel the hand. Fine cotton should feel smooth and cool, not stiff or papery.
  • Check the seams. On shirts, look for narrow, flat-felled or French seams with small stitches.
  • Read the label. Long-staple cotton varieties generally produce smoother, stronger yarns.
  • Mind the finish. Heavily starched or coated fabrics may feel very different after the first wash.

Calendering

Pressing cloth between heated rollers to flatten and smooth it, giving cambric its characteristic soft lustre.

Mercerisation

A treatment that makes cotton stronger, more lustrous and better at absorbing dye.

Long-staple cotton

Cotton with longer fibres, which spin into finer, smoother and more durable yarn.
